Help and FAQ

How to connect the C69 M Star, use the app, and fix the usual problems.

Connect the tablet

  1. On the tablet, open Apps, tap the menu at the top right, then App management.
  2. Turn on USB Debug Mode.
  3. Connect the tablet to your computer with a USB cable that carries data. Some charging-only cables don't.
  4. When the tablet asks Allow USB debugging?, tap Allow and tick Always allow from this computer.
  5. Back in the app, the dot next to Connected fills in. Press Refresh to check straight away.

If the app says Install Android platform tools, adb is missing. Press Download Android platform tools on that screen. It fetches Google's official tools into the app's own folder, with no admin rights needed. Or download it from Google, then press Refresh.

Linux

Download linux_amd64.tar.gz (or linux_arm64.tar.gz on ARM) from the latest release, unpack it with tar -xzf, and run ./boox-debloat.

Linux also needs permission to use the tablet over USB. Install adb and its USB rules, then log out and back in:

  • Debian, Ubuntu, Mint: sudo apt install adb android-sdk-platform-tools-common, then sudo usermod -aG plugdev $USER
  • Fedora: sudo dnf install android-tools
  • Arch, Manjaro: sudo pacman -S android-tools android-udev

If the app says Linux needs permission to use the tablet, the USB rules aren't active yet. Log out and back in, run adb kill-server, and replug the tablet.

Use the app

  1. Choose apps. Switch off the apps you want removed, or use Remove all. Apps under Always kept can't be removed.
  2. Review changes, check the list, then Apply to tablet. Your app list is saved as a backup first.
  3. Restart the tablet when it asks, or any time with Restart tablet.

To bring an app back, switch it on again and apply, or use Restore all apps.

Install apps

Choose Apps in the left panel.

  1. Under Recommended for e-ink, open an app's official page: F-Droid or the developer's GitHub releases.
  2. Download the .apk file. When a page offers several, pick the one for your tablet's processor (shown on the Apps page) or the universal one.
  3. Drop the APK onto Install an app, or choose it. The app installs, or updates if it's already there.

Your apps lists the apps you installed, with an Uninstall button for each. System apps aren't listed there; Debloat handles those.

Only .apk files work. Split bundles (.apks, .xapk) don't. The recommended list links only to official sources, not to APK mirror sites, which can carry modified apps.

Files

Choose Files in the left panel to browse the tablet's storage.

  • Open a folder by its name. The path at the top takes you back.
  • Upload files, or drop files on the list, to copy them into the folder you're in. Books go in Books.
  • Download copies a file to your computer's downloads.
  • Delete removes a file, or a folder once it's empty. It asks first, and it can't be undone.

Only the tablet's shared storage is shown. The Android folder can be browsed but not changed, because apps keep their data there.

Sleep picture

  1. Drop a picture onto the drawing of the tablet, or use Choose picture.
  2. Keep Fit to screen on. The picture is resized to 758 × 1024, the tablet's screen size.
  3. Keep Set it as the sleep picture on, and choose Set sleep picture.
  4. Don't touch the tablet for about 30 seconds. The app taps through the tablet's menus for you.

If it can't finish, set it by hand: on the tablet, open Storage, then Image (or Pictures), long-press the picture, tap Set Screensaver, then Set as screensaver and OK.

Set it from the file browser. The hidden Wallpaper Settings page crashes on this model.

Google Play

If the left panel says Google Play: Play Store missing, your firmware came without the Play Store. Download Google's Play Store APK (the nodpi version), for example from APKMirror, and choose it with Install Play Store. The app only installs it if it's signed by Google's Play Store key, or by the same key as the Google apps on your tablet. Choose a plain .apk, not an XAPK bundle.

If it says Google Play: off, press Turn on Google Play, then restart the tablet. It only turns on the parts that are off.

The Star Education firmware isn't certified by Google, so Play Store may say This device isn't Play Protect certified. Debloating doesn't cause it: the app never removes Google Play, Play services or Google Services Framework.

  1. Sign in to a Google account on the tablet, or open Play Store once.
  2. Get the tablet's GSF ID. In Terminal or PowerShell, run: 
    adb shell 'content query --uri content://com.google.android.gsf.gservices --where "name=\"android_id\""'
    The long number after value= is the GSF ID.
  3. Open google.com/android/uncertified, sign in with the same account, paste the ID and choose Register.
  4. Wait 10 to 30 minutes. It can take a few hours.
  5. On the tablet, open Settings, then Apps. For Google Play Store and Google Play services, tap Force stop, then open Storage and tap Clear data.
  6. Restart the tablet, open Play Store and sign in again.

Turn off Play Protect scanning

Once Play Store works, Play Protect may warn about or block apps you install yourself, like Aurora Store or KOReader. To stop that: open Play Store, tap your profile picture, then Play Protect, then the settings (gear) icon, and turn off Scan apps with Play Protect. You can turn it back on the same way.

Clearing Play services' data signs you out of Google on the tablet. A factory reset or firmware update gives the tablet a new GSF ID, so register again after one.

Questions

Is removing apps safe? Can I undo it?

Yes. Apps are removed only for your user with pm uninstall --user 0. Nothing is deleted from the tablet, and every app comes back with its switch or with Restore all apps. A factory reset also brings them all back.

Why can't I remove the apps under Always kept?

The sleep screen, firmware updates and Google Play need them. Removing them breaks those features, so the app only lets you bring them back.

What is Onyx sync (ksync), and why does it matter?

Older debloat guides removed it. Without it, setting a sleep picture hangs on Processing… or doesn't work. If the app shows it under Always kept with Bring back, use that, apply, and restart the tablet.

Do the Android test components affect Play Protect?

No. The two Android test components are empty placeholders used by Google's compatibility tests. Play Protect certification depends on the firmware and the GSF ID registration. See Google Play.

The app says "Plug in your tablet" and nothing happens

Check that USB Debug Mode is on and that the cable carries data, then press Refresh. Try a different USB port. Running adb devices in a terminal shows whether your computer can see the tablet.

It says "Waiting for USB permission"

Unlock the tablet and tap Allow on the prompt. If you don't see one, unplug the tablet and plug it in again.

Linux says it needs permission to use the tablet

adb can see the tablet but isn't allowed to open it. Install the USB rules for your distro (see Linux), log out and back in, run adb kill-server, then unplug and replug the tablet.

Windows doesn't see the tablet

Install the Google USB driver, then unplug and replug the tablet.

It says "This page has expired"

Each run of boox-debloat opens a new private address. Close the page and run boox-debloat again.

An app from Play Store closes straight away

Some apps run Google's licence check, which can fail on uncertified firmware. Install the app from Aurora Store or the developer's own site instead.

An APK won't install

The app explains why. The usual causes: a version from a different developer is already installed (uninstall it first), the app needs a newer Android than 11, or the APK is for a different processor.

Where are the backups?

On a Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/boox-debloat/backups. On Windows, in %AppData%\boox-debloat\backups. A new one is saved before every change.

Report a problem

  1. To watch what the app does as it happens, choose Show activity in the left panel.
  2. In the app, choose Copy logs in the left panel. It copies what the app did and what the tablet answered.
  3. Open a new issue on GitHub, say what you tried, and paste the logs.

The logs stay on your computer until you paste them. They show only the last four characters of the tablet's serial number and hide your user folder.
